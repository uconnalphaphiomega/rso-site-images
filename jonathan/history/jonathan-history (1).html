The Jonathans

Fifteen Huskies, one name, and more than ninety years of UConn tradition.

Where the name comes from

Since 1935, a live Husky named Jonathan has walked the UConn campus, greeting students and bringing back memories for alumni. Every Jonathan is named for Jonathan Trumbull, the last colonial governor and first state governor of Connecticut. Fifteen have carried the name so far, and each holds a special place in UConn history.

1970

APO takes the leash

In 1969, the Student Senate voted to stop funding the live mascot, saying Jonathan “represented the establishment.” Alpha Phi Omega stepped in with a campus-wide poll, and the answer was clear: keep Jonathan. Since 1970, APO brothers have cared for him as our signature service project, from March Madness games to his daily walks around campus.

Their stories

 

2024 – Present

Jonathan XV

  • Born April 19, 2023 in Atwood, Ontario, Canada, at Kybra Kennels.
  • Introduced to the UConn community on June 28, 2023, and trained alongside his big brother, Jonathan XIV.
  • Stepped into his official role as active mascot on March 3, 2024, and has been dutifully serving ever since.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

2014 – 2024

Jonathan XIV

  • Born October 5, 2013 in El Dorado, Arkansas, the largest of seven puppies.
  • Introduced to campus on January 29, 2014. His black-and-white coat matched UConn’s updated athletics logo.
  • A famously fluffy goof, he traveled with the men’s basketball team to the 2023 national championship.
  • Retired in March 2024.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

2008 – 2014

Jonathan XIII

  • Born March 2008 in Hartstown, Pennsylvania, after a three-year pause while the live mascot program was rebuilt.
  • Retired as mascot duties grew more demanding, then spent seven years as Jonathan XIV’s companion.
  • Passed away in August 2021.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

2005 – 2008

A pause to rebuild. UConn went three years without a live mascot while the program’s policies were completely revamped.

 

2001 – 2005

Jonathan XII

  • Debuted in 2001.
  • Large crowds weren’t his favorite, so he made fewer public appearances than most Jonathans.
  • Retired in 2005.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1995 – 2001

Jonathan XI

  • His arrival was announced in May 1995, two weeks after the Husky statue was unveiled.
  • Known for chasing ducks into Mirror Lake.
  • Retired in 2001 and went on to serve as a therapy dog.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1991 – 1995

Jonathan X

  • Born in New Jersey and arrived in July 1991. He was briefly announced as Jonathan IX before the record was corrected.
  • Had two different-colored eyes, a trait called heterochromia.
  • Lost in an accident in February 1995. The Husky statue near Gampel Pavilion was unveiled in his memory.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1979 – 1991

Jonathan IX

  • Began his service in October 1979. Some university records list him as Jonathan VIII.
  • Recognized as the official University Mascot by the Board of Trustees in 1989.
  • The first dog to enter Gampel Pavilion, in 1990. Passed away in 1991.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1977 – 1979

Jonathan VIII

  • Born in 1977.
  • Ended his service in 1979 at three years old and stayed with his family.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1964 – 1977

Jonathan VII

  • Born in 1964 and, like Jonathan VI, flown in from the University of Alaska.
  • Considered a lucky charm, he was on hand when UConn beat Yale in football.
  • In 1969, the Student Senate voted to stop funding the live mascot, and a switch to a Yankee mascot was floated (and widely rejected). APO ran a campus-wide poll to keep him and took over his care in 1970.
  • Served until 1977 and became the first Jonathan named Mascot Emeritus.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1963 – 1964

Jonathan VI

  • Born in 1963. The Class of 1967 was tasked with finding him, and the first Husky Committee, chaired by Karen Fromkin Wheeler ’67, arranged his flight from the University of Alaska.
  • Lived with Trumbull House in Towers.
  • Served until 1964 and rests alongside Jonathan IV beneath Husky Village.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1963

Homer the Husky. After Jonathan V retired, a costumed mascot named Homer the Husky cheered on the Huskies until Jonathan VI arrived.

 

1959 – 1963

Jonathan V

  • Born June 1958, a purebred Alaskan Malamute.
  • The Class of 1961 held a Jonathan-themed Junior Ball, with proceeds going to his care.
  • Loud crowds were hard on him, and he retired in 1963.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1949 – 1959

Jonathan IV

  • Born in 1947, half Husky and half Malamute, and first named Ya Rov.
  • Attended UConn’s first NCAA basketball tournament, at Madison Square Garden in 1951, and once raided the University of Rhode Island’s chicken coop during a game.
  • Kidnapped by UMass Amherst in 1956. In 1958, APO built him a new kennel as a service project.
  • In 1958 it became clear he needed to retire, and he passed away in January 1959. He rests near his kennel in Husky Village.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1947 – 1949

Jonathan III

  • Born in 1945 and debuted in November 1947. Before UConn, he trained as a sled dog for the Navy’s Operation Highjump to the South Pole.
  • Best known for chasing Handsome Dan, Yale’s bulldog, around the Yale Bowl.
  • Dog-napped by UConn’s own New London campus before a football game against Storrs. Served until 1949.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1935 – 1947

Jonathan II

  • A cousin of Jonathan I and the first all-white Jonathan.
  • After UConn beat Brown 27–0 in September 1936, he chased Brown’s live bear mascot up a tree.
  • One of the longest-serving Jonathans, he served until 1947.

↑ All 15 Jonathans

 
 

1935

Jonathan I

  • After the University of Rhode Island’s live ram mascot was kidnapped in 1934, UConn wanted a live mascot of its own.
  • Born in July 1934 in Huntington, Connecticut, to a family of Arctic expedition sled dogs and performing dogs. He lived with the university’s Music Director and his four children.
  • Named in an Alumni Association contest, where “Jonathan” beat out Laurel, Rover, and Constacol.
